Glove Pose Estimator

How we get from raw tracker, IMU and servo readings to joint angles and fingertip poses of the operator's hand. One small EKF per finger, built on a hinge-chain hand model, with the Vive tracker giving the palm orientation.

1Overview 2Hand model 3Sensors 4Fusion 5EKF 6Live filter 7Thumb 8Calibration 9Limits 10Literature

01Overview

Four sensor streams come in on two clocks. The tracker pose arrives on the PC through SteamVR; the hand IMU, finger IMUs and servo readings arrive from the ESP32 in the glove state frame. A small palm filter turns tracker plus hand IMU into a clean 500 Hz palm orientation. With that orientation known, each finger becomes an independent problem, so each gets its own EKF with only 9 states.

What comes out: joint angles and rates for thumb, index and middle, their covariance, and fingertip positions and pad normals in the palm frame and in the sim world. The covariance is useful downstream. When spread is unobservable (palm down, see section 5), its uncertainty grows, and retargeting can fall back to a neutral spread instead of trusting a drifting value.

Finger chain (index, middle)

Each finger is a planar chain hanging off a 2-DoF knuckle. The knuckle has spread \(\alpha\) about \(z_H\) and flexion \(\theta_1\) about the new y axis. Flexion is positive when the finger curls toward the palm.

\[ T_{HP}(\alpha,\theta_1) = \mathrm{Trans}(m_i)\,R_z(\alpha)\,R_y(\theta_1), \qquad T_{P,\mathrm{tip}} = \mathrm{Trans}(L_1\hat x)\,R_y(\theta_2)\,\mathrm{Trans}(L_2\hat x)\,R_y(\theta_3)\,\mathrm{Trans}(L_3\hat x) \]

Here \(m_i\) is the knuckle position in the palm frame and \(L_1, L_2, L_3\) are the segment lengths. The DIP joint is not independent in normal motion; it follows the PIP joint closely enough that the usual model is

\[ \theta_3 \approx c\,\theta_2, \qquad c \approx 0.67 \]

So the estimated joint vector per finger is \(q = [\alpha, \theta_1, \theta_2]\) and the fingertip is \(p^H_\mathrm{tip} = \mathrm{FK}(q)\), \(p^W_\mathrm{tip} = T_{WH}\,p^H_\mathrm{tip}\). The pad normal is the \(-z\) axis of the distal frame.

03Sensors: from raw value to physical quantity

For each sensor: what the hardware gives us, how we convert it, and the measurement model \(h(\cdot)\) that predicts the reading from the state. That last part is what the EKF uses.

Vive tracker

Raw
OpenVR mDeviceToAbsoluteTracking, a 3×4 pose of the tracker in the SteamVR frame V. SteamVR already fuses the tracker's own IMU with the lighthouse sweeps.
Physical value
\(T_{WT} = T_{WV}\,T_{VT}\), then the palm \(T_{WH} = T_{WT}\,T_{TH}\). Both transforms already exist in tracking.py and the calibration file.
Tells us
Palm position and full orientation in W, without drift.
Errors
Latency of a few ms on the PC clock, jumps on occlusion, small jitter. With one base station, occlusion by the other hand happens.

Hand IMU

Raw
int16 gyro and accel counts per axis, read by the ESP at 500 Hz, timestamped on the ESP clock.
Physical value
\(\omega = \frac{\text{raw}}{s_g}\cdot\frac{\pi}{180} - b_g\), \(f = \frac{\text{raw}}{s_a}\,g - b_a\). At ±2000 °/s a typical chip has \(s_g \approx 16.4\) LSB per °/s; at ±8 g, \(s_a \approx 4096\) LSB per g. Check the datasheet of the chip we pick.
Tells us
Palm angular rate on the same clock as the finger IMUs. That makes the relative finger rate clean, and cross-correlating it with the tracker's rate gives the PC–ESP time offset.
Errors
Gyro bias (temperature dependent), noise. Its own orientation drifts, but the tracker corrects that in the palm filter.

Finger IMUs

Raw
Same as the hand IMU, one per finger on the proximal segment.
Physical value
Gyro: relative rate \(\omega_{rel} = \omega_{S} - R_{SS_h}\,\omega_{S_h}\), with \(R_{SS_h}\) from the current state. Accel: specific force \(f_S\), which at low acceleration is gravity seen in S.
Model
\(h_\omega = R_{PS}^\top\big(R_y(\theta_1)^\top\hat z\,\dot\alpha + \hat y\,\dot\theta_1\big) + b\)
\(h_f = R_{PS}^\top R_y(\theta_1)^\top R_z(\alpha)^\top R_{WH}^\top\,[0,0,g]^\top\)
Tells us
α̇ and θ̇₁ always; α and θ₁ in absolute terms only through gravity, which depends on how the palm is held.
Errors
Gyro bias, accel corrupted by motion, mount misalignment, skin and strap motion.

Servo / cable

Raw
XL330 present position (4096 ticks per revolution, 0.088° per tick) and present current.
Physical value
\(\Delta l = r_s\,\frac{2\pi}{4096}(n - n_0)\) with spool radius \(r_s\). Torque from current via a calibrated constant, \(\tau = k_t\,i\).
Model
\(h_l = r_1\theta_1 + (r_2 + c\,r_3)\,\theta_2 + \tau/k_c\)
\(r_j\): effective moment arm at joint j. \(k_c\): tendon + strap stiffness, from the fingertip force test.
Tells us
A weighted sum of flexion, in every hand pose, without drift.
Errors
Stretch under load (handled by \(\tau/k_c\)), strap slip, slack when the servo doesn't keep the tendon taut.

04How the fusion is split

Palm filter. Propagate \(R_{WH}\) with the hand gyro at 500 Hz and correct it toward the tracker pose, delayed by the estimated time offset. A complementary filter or a small error-state filter on SO(3) both work. The tracker pose alone would also do for a first version, since SteamVR already fuses its IMU. The point of the palm filter is having \(R_{WH}\) on the ESP clock, so the accel model of every finger uses a palm orientation from the same instant.

Finger EKFs. Given \(R_{WH}\), the fingers don't share any unknowns, so the joint estimation splits into one filter per finger. Each one is 9 states, and the measurement models are a handful of sines and cosines. That is cheap enough to run in the Python sim loop at 500 Hz; the ESP only forwards raw IMU data in the state frame (about 24 kB/s for four IMUs).

Why an EKF. The measurement models are nonlinear (rotations), but only mildly over one 2 ms step, and the state is small. A complementary filter per joint would be simpler but can't combine the cable sum with the IMU angle cleanly or estimate bias. A UKF or a sliding-window optimizer would also work; the EKF is the easiest to debug and the natural first choice.

05The finger EKF in detail

State

\[ x = \big[\;\underbrace{\alpha,\;\theta_1,\;\theta_2}_{q}\;,\;\underbrace{\dot\alpha,\;\dot\theta_1,\;\dot\theta_2}_{\dot q}\;,\;\underbrace{b_x,\;b_y,\;b_z}_{b_g}\;\big]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^9 \]

The gyro bias here is the bias of the relative rate. The hand IMU's own bias is folded in, which is fine since it is slow too.

Process model

Constant velocity for the joints, random walk for the bias. With step \(\Delta t = 2\) ms:

\[ q_{k+1} = q_k + \Delta t\,\dot q_k,\qquad \dot q_{k+1} = \dot q_k + w_v,\qquad b_{k+1} = b_k + w_b \]
\[ F = \begin{bmatrix} I_3 & \Delta t\,I_3 & 0\\ 0 & I_3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & I_3\end{bmatrix},\qquad Q = \mathrm{diag}\big(0_3,\;\sigma_v^2\Delta t\,I_3,\;\sigma_b^2\Delta t\,I_3\big) \]
\[ \hat x^-_{k+1} = F\hat x_k,\qquad P^-_{k+1} = F P_k F^\top + Q \]

\(\sigma_v\) is how fast joint velocity may change (angular acceleration noise). Fingers are quick, so it is large; the gyro then dominates the velocity.

Updates

All three measurements use the same standard step, with \(H = \partial h / \partial x\) at the predicted state:

\[ y = z - h(\hat x^-),\quad S = H P^- H^\top + R,\quad K = P^- H^\top S^{-1},\quad \hat x = \hat x^- + K y,\quad P = (I - KH)P^-(I-KH)^\top + KRK^\top \]

Gyro. In the proximal frame, the relative rate is

\[ \omega^P_{rel} = R_y(\theta_1)^\top\hat z\,\dot\alpha + \hat y\,\dot\theta_1 = \begin{bmatrix} -\sin\theta_1\,\dot\alpha \\ \dot\theta_1 \\ \cos\theta_1\,\dot\alpha\end{bmatrix}, \qquad h_\omega = R_{PS}^\top\,\omega^P_{rel} + b \]
\[ \frac{\partial h_\omega}{\partial \theta_1} = R_{PS}^\top\begin{bmatrix}-\cos\theta_1\,\dot\alpha\\0\\-\sin\theta_1\,\dot\alpha\end{bmatrix},\quad \frac{\partial h_\omega}{\partial \dot\alpha} = R_{PS}^\top\begin{bmatrix}-\sin\theta_1\\0\\\cos\theta_1\end{bmatrix},\quad \frac{\partial h_\omega}{\partial \dot\theta_1} = R_{PS}^\top\,\hat y,\quad \frac{\partial h_\omega}{\partial b} = I_3 \]

The finger can only turn in the plane spanned by the α̇ and θ̇₁ directions. The rate component orthogonal to both is pure bias, so that bias axis is always observable. This is what the earlier "hinge constraint" means in filter terms. θ̇₂ is not measured by this IMU at all.

Cable. Linear, so the Jacobian is exact:

\[ h_l = r_1\theta_1 + (r_2 + c\,r_3)\,\theta_2, \qquad z_l = \Delta l - \tau/k_c, \qquad H_l = [\,0,\; r_1,\; r_2 + c\,r_3,\; 0,\dots,0\,] \]

With the calibrated curve instead of constant moment arms, \(H_l\) is the local slope.

Accelerometer. Let \(g^H = R_{WH}^\top[0,0,g]^\top\) (from the palm filter) and \(w = R_z(\alpha)^\top g^H\). Then

\[ h_f = R_{PS}^\top R_y(\theta_1)^\top w, \qquad \frac{\partial h_f}{\partial \theta_1} = R_{PS}^\top\big(h^P_f \times \hat y\big), \qquad \frac{\partial h_f}{\partial \alpha} = -R_{PS}^\top R_y(\theta_1)^\top\big(\hat z \times w\big) \]

The second Jacobian is the observability result in one line. Palm down, \(g^H\) is parallel to \(\hat z\), so \(\hat z \times w = 0\) and the accelerometer has nothing to say about α. In the handshake pose \(g^H\) is parallel to \(\hat y\) and the first Jacobian vanishes instead.

Gating the accelerometer

The accel model assumes it measures only gravity. During fast motion it doesn't, so we skip the update when

\[ \big|\,\|f\| - g\,\big| \gt \epsilon_f \quad(\epsilon_f \approx 0.5\text{–}1\ \text{m/s}^2) \qquad\text{or}\qquad y^\top S^{-1} y \gt \chi^2_{3,\,0.99} \approx 11.3 \]

The norm check misses accelerations that happen to keep \(\|f\|\) near g, so the Mahalanobis gate catches the rest. An alternative is to inflate \(R_f\) with the measured \(\big|\|f\|-g\big|\) instead of a hard cut.

Joint limits

After each update, clip q to its limits and zero the velocity component pointing out of range. This is the crude version of constrained Kalman filtering; the cleaner versions project the estimate or truncate the Gaussian at the bound (Simon 2010). Clipping is enough to start and keeps spread from wandering off palm-down.

Stillness updates

When the relative gyro norm and accel variance stay small for about 0.3 s, add a pseudo-measurement \(\dot q = 0\) with small noise. Then the gyro reading equals the bias, which pins down the on-hinge bias axes too. Do this at startup and in every pause.

Code sketch

About the size of the real thing. NumPy, no allocation tricks needed at this state size.

import numpy as np


def rot_y(t):
    """Rotation about y."""
    c, s = np.cos(t), np.sin(t)
    return np.array([[c, 0, s], [0, 1, 0], [-s, 0, c]])


def rot_z(t):
    """Rotation about z."""
    c, s = np.cos(t), np.sin(t)
    return np.array([[c, -s, 0], [s, c, 0], [0, 0, 1]])


class FingerEKF:
    """EKF for one finger with state [alpha, th1, th2, dalpha, dth1, dth2, bx, by, bz]."""

    def __init__(self, p):
        """Set initial state, covariance, noise and finger parameters."""
        self.x = np.zeros(9)
        self.P = np.diag(p.p0)
        self.q_vel, self.q_bias = p.q_vel, p.q_bias
        self.R_PS = p.R_PS
        self.r1, self.k2 = p.r1, p.r2 + p.c * p.r3
        self.lo, self.hi = np.asarray(p.lower), np.asarray(p.upper)

    def predict(self, dt):
        """Propagate with a constant-velocity model."""
        F = np.eye(9)
        F[0:3, 3:6] = dt * np.eye(3)
        Q = np.diag([0, 0, 0] + [self.q_vel * dt] * 3 + [self.q_bias * dt] * 3)
        self.x = F @ self.x
        self.P = F @ self.P @ F.T + Q

    def update(self, z, h, H, R, gate=None):
        """Joseph-form EKF update with optional Mahalanobis gate."""
        y = z - h
        S = H @ self.P @ H.T + R
        if gate is not None and y @ np.linalg.solve(S, y) > gate:
            return False
        K = self.P @ H.T @ np.linalg.inv(S)
        self.x = self.x + K @ y
        A = np.eye(9) - K @ H
        self.P = A @ self.P @ A.T + K @ R @ K.T
        self.clamp()
        return True

    def gyro(self, w_rel, R):
        """Update with the relative angular rate in the IMU frame."""
        t1, da, dt1 = self.x[1], self.x[3], self.x[4]
        s, c = np.sin(t1), np.cos(t1)
        h = self.R_PS.T @ np.array([-s * da, dt1, c * da]) + self.x[6:9]
        H = np.zeros((3, 9))
        H[:, 1] = self.R_PS.T @ np.array([-c * da, 0, -s * da])
        H[:, 3] = self.R_PS.T @ np.array([-s, 0, c])
        H[:, 4] = self.R_PS.T @ np.array([0, 1, 0])
        H[:, 6:9] = np.eye(3)
        return self.update(w_rel, h, H, R)

    def cable(self, dl, tau, k_c, R):
        """Update with tendon length change corrected for stretch."""
        H = np.zeros((1, 9))
        H[0, 1], H[0, 2] = self.r1, self.k2
        return self.update(np.array([dl - tau / k_c]), H @ self.x, H, R)

    def accel(self, f, g_H, R, eps=0.6, gate=11.3):
        """Update with the gravity direction when the specific force is close to g."""
        if abs(np.linalg.norm(f) - 9.81) > eps:
            return False
        Ry = rot_y(self.x[1])
        w = rot_z(self.x[0]).T @ g_H
        h_P = Ry.T @ w
        H = np.zeros((3, 9))
        H[:, 0] = -Ry.T @ np.cross([0, 0, 1], w)
        H[:, 1] = np.cross(h_P, [0, 1, 0])
        return self.update(f, self.R_PS.T @ h_P, self.R_PS.T @ H, R, gate)

    def still(self, R):
        """Zero-velocity pseudo-measurement while the hand rests."""
        H = np.zeros((3, 9))
        H[:, 3:6] = np.eye(3)
        return self.update(np.zeros(3), self.x[3:6], H, R)

    def clamp(self):
        """Clip joint angles to their limits and stop motion past them."""
        for i in range(3):
            if not self.lo[i] <= self.x[i] <= self.hi[i]:
                self.x[i] = np.clip(self.x[i], self.lo[i], self.hi[i])
                self.x[i + 3] = 0.0

07Thumb

The thumb doesn't fit the hinge-chain template. Its base joint (CMC) has two non-orthogonal axes, and opposition is mostly a rotation there.

\[ R_{HP_\mathrm{th}} = R_{H,\mathrm{CMC}}\;R_z(\beta_2)\,R_y(\beta_1)\;R_{\mathrm{off}}\;R_y(\beta_3), \qquad h_l = r_3\beta_3 + r_4\beta_4 \;(+\,r_1\beta_1 \text{ if the cable crosses CMC}) \]

The IMU segment now turns with three joint angles, so its relative orientation to the palm is fully 3D. Same filter structure, 4 angles, 4 rates, 3 biases. Two differences:

  • No hinge constraint. All three gyro axes carry motion, so the bias is only observable through gravity, the cable and stillness updates. Heading about gravity drifts until one of those helps.
  • β₁ vs β₃ is weakly separated. Both bend the thumb toward the palm through similar axes. The joint limits and a prior that couples them (they usually move together in grasping) help. If it turns out too weak in the rig, a second IMU on the thumb metacarpal is the clean fix.

For the class project this is the riskiest part of the estimator, so I'd get index and middle working first, then the thumb.

08Calibration

Once per person and per glove fit. The order matters, since later steps use earlier results.

  1. Gyro bias at rest. Hand still on the table for 2 s. Average the gyro.
  2. Flat hand, palm down. Defines q = 0 and the cable zero \(n_0\). Gravity in each IMU gives two of three mount angles of \(R_{PS}\).
  3. Curl each finger alone (MCP only, as much as possible). The dominant gyro axis is the knuckle axis \(\hat y\) in S. Together with step 2 this fixes \(R_{PS}\) completely.
  4. Fist, hook, pinches (thumb to index tip, thumb to middle tip). Fit the cable curve \(l(\theta_1, \theta_2)\) by least squares, using θ₁ from the IMU. Pinches also tune thumb parameters so FK puts the tips together.
  5. Segment lengths. From a ruler on the hand, or one scale factor on a standard hand.
  6. Time offset. Rotate the hand quickly for a few seconds. Cross-correlate the hand gyro rate with the rate from successive tracker poses.
  7. Tendon stiffness \(k_c\). From the fingertip force rig: commanded torque vs measured Δl with the finger blocked.

Steps 1 to 3 take under a minute and should be repeated when the glove is put on again. Steps 4 to 7 are per person or per hardware change.

09Known limits

  • Spread with the palm down is gyro only, bounded by limits. Its σ tells retargeting when not to trust it.
  • Model error dominates once sensors are good: wrong segment lengths, moment arms and coupling factor c directly bias the tips. Expect fingertip errors of roughly a centimetre, not millimetres, without careful calibration.
  • Skin and strap motion. The IMU sits on soft tissue and moves relative to the bone during grasping.
  • Cable during force rendering. \(\tau/k_c\) handles linear stretch; friction in the guides adds hysteresis that a single constant won't capture.
  • Slack. In free motion the servo must keep the tendon taut, or Δl is meaningless. The rig's taut-tendon mode is exactly this.
  • Ring and pinky are copied from the middle finger.
